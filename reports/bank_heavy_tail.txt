2. HAS THE VaR BEEN HONEST? One-day 99% VaR set each evening, against the next
   day's actual marked P&L
   Only days on which every underlying in the book was valued are tested (123 of 127): on the
   others some files had not started or had ended, and the book was a part of itself.
   123 days tested; VaR averaged 48,114 (from 20,446 to 79,838).
   Days the loss went past it: 5, where a correct 99% VaR expects 1.2.
   Those days: 08 Jul 2026 (loss 45,460 against 41,999), 20 Jul 2026 (loss 58,555 against 44,987), 29 Jul 2026 (loss 78,449 against 44,854), 19 Aug 2026 (loss 93,335 against 51,672), 07 Oct 2026 (loss 72,140 against 57,293)
   Kupiec's test: statistic 6.60, p-value 0.01. The count is not chance: the VaR is mis-sized.
   Basel traffic light: YELLOW (a correct model shows this many or fewer 99.84% of the time; yellow starts at 95%, red at 99.99%).
   Read with care: 123 days cannot tell a 99% VaR from a 98% one. The VaR here
   prices each evening's book through its delta, gamma and vega, not in full. And a
   window of the 250 days before knows only the market those days had: when moves
   get larger than that year's, a historical VaR is too small until it catches up.
   Over these days the book's actual daily st. dev. was 27,002; the windows behind the VaR implied about 20,685.
